How I work

Experienced with the full data lifecycle: from collection to communication

Many analysts are skilled with code, able to manipulate and transform data to glean valuable insights. I enjoy deriving meaning from numbers too — but that is where the work starts, not where it ends. Across the collegiate and professional ranks of baseball I have hands-on experience with Trackman, Rapsodo, Edgertronic, BATS, Synergy, and more, having collected that data firsthand.

That background has also taught me the game at a high level, which helps me distill the information down to what actually matters. And working closely with coaches and players means I know how to communicate a finding not just to other analysts, but to the people playing the game.

This combination — understanding material in depth and communicating it well — applies well beyond the field. It transfers to the markets and to other sectors of business, which is where the second half of my background points.

What separates an elite curveball

2024 MLB season  ·  R  ·  k-means clustering

Clustering

Context

Curveballs were the second-most thrown off-speed pitch of the 2024 season, and the only common pitch built primarily on downward movement. Pitch-design models like Stuff+ grade pitches in isolation; we wanted to know which curveball traits actually produce run value, measured against each pitcher’s own fastball.

My role

Student analyst, one of three. Helped select the nine clustering features across usage, independent outcomes, and movement relative to each pitcher’s fastball, then clustered the season into eight groups and examined how handedness cut across them.

Outcome

Two of the eight clusters separated clearly on run value — one almost entirely left-handed, one almost entirely right-handed. Both led the league in whiff rate, and the biggest divider was horizontal break relative to the fastball, not vertical, which is the opposite of what a pitch that moves downward would suggest. Usage cut against intuition too: the highest-usage cluster threw curveballs 40% of the time and returned middling value.

Predicting NFL spreads and money lines

2010–2025 play-by-play  ·  R  ·  team of five

Predictive modeling

Context

Sportsbooks set the most rigorously tested forecasts in sports. We asked whether a model built on public play-by-play data could predict NFL point differentials more accurately than DraftKings — a benchmark that is unusually honest, because the market has already priced in everything widely known.

My role

One of five. I ran exploratory analysis on conversion rates and situational stats, built the random forest baseline the final model was measured against, and co-wrote the report. The team engineered 341 rolling-average features and settled on a tuned XGBoost.

Outcome

Trained on 2010–2022 and tested on held-out 2023–2025 seasons, the model hit 77.4% against the spread and 74.1% on the money line across 594 simulated wagers, landing closer to the true point differential than the DraftKings line 65.8% of the time. SHAP attribution put defensive and special-teams variables at the top — sack rate, opponent plays, net punt yards — ahead of the offensive metrics we expected.

Reading the market

Where baseball analytics is going

Baseball is repricing two things at once. On the field, a rule change is taking value away from a skill clubs spent a decade learning to measure. Off it, a labor negotiation could put a hard ceiling on payroll for the first time in the sport’s history. Both land on the same desk — the group that has to say what a player is worth. That is the desk I want to sit at, and the reason I have built a background in valuation as well as in baseball.

The strike zone became a measurement

The ABS Challenge System is in place for the full 2026 season — spring training, regular season, and postseason. In spring testing catchers won 56% of their challenges, the highest of any position, which is precisely the group whose framing value the system puts at risk. A decade of pitch-framing metrics now describes a smaller edge than it did.

Where I fit: I have collected tracking data by hand across four seasons. When a measurement system changes, the first question is what the new data can and cannot support — and that question is answered by people who know how the number is captured, not only how to model it.

MLB.com, September 2025 · ESPN, September 2025

Payroll may stop being elastic

The collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. on December 1, 2026, with a salary cap as the central point of contention and a lockout widely expected. If a cap arrives, roster construction stops being a spending question and becomes a constrained allocation problem: fixed budget, competing assets, uncertain returns.

Where I fit: that is portfolio construction with a different asset class. My finance minor and investment club work were spent valuing long-horizon positions under constraint, which is the same arithmetic a capped front office would run on every contract.

ESPN, December 2025 · Sportico, July 2026

The hiring is not only at clubs

Listing data from spring 2026 showed betting and data-infrastructure companies posting more open analytics roles than clubs did — FanDuel and Genius Sports each well ahead of any single team. Club analytics groups remain small and competitive; the surrounding industry that sells clubs their data is where volume is growing.

Where I fit: the vendors build the tools, but the clubs decide what matters. Having worked at the collegiate, Triple-A, and Major League levels, I know what a coaching staff will actually use — which is the harder half of the problem on either side of that line.
