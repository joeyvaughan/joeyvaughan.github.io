How I work

Most analysts see the data after it exists. I have been on both ends of it.

I have fed pitching machines and caught bullpens, and I have written the SQL that turns a season of those bullpens into a scouting report. That combination is the thing I lead with, because the failure mode in this field is rarely the modeling — it is an analyst who does not know how the number was captured, or who cannot get a coaching staff to act on it once it is built.

The four capabilities below come from outside a strict analytics job description — from print deadlines, equity valuation, historical research, and a field tarp. Each one transfers, and each one transfers twice: into a baseball front office, and into the financial analysis work I am pursuing in parallel.

Predicting NFL spreads and money lines

2010–2025 play-by-play  ·  four model families compared

Predictive modeling

Context

Betting markets are the hardest available benchmark for a forecasting model: the line already contains the crowd’s information, so beating it means finding something the market has not priced.

My role

Sole analyst. Built and compared linear regression, lasso, random forest, and XGBoost against fifteen seasons of play-by-play and schedule data, then selected XGBoost on out-of-sample performance.
